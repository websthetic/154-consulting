body:
  - The school has real history behind it, so the crest had to look inherited rather than commissioned. An open laurel wreath, a split year, a maple leaf worked into the foot. Navy against a warm gold, Georgia set against Montserrat. It works as a seal and as a badge, which is what a school actually needs.
  - We wrote a photography standard into the guidelines too, because the thing that usually breaks a school brand is the imagery that arrives after handover.
  - The site had to separate the academic story from the basketball programme without making them feel like two different schools.
  - A crest is the hardest kind of mark to rebuild, because everyone at the school already knows what it looks like and nobody has the file. It was redrawn contour by contour against the best artwork that survived, then specified in four colourways with the usage rules shown rather than described, so a teacher making a flyer at nine at night cannot get it wrong by accident.
story:
  caps:
    - <b>A school founded in 1997.</b> Nearly thirty years of crest, used everywhere from a blazer badge to a website header, with no version that held together at the small end.
    - <b>Set out before drawn.</b> The ring, the laurel and the banner each get their own radius. A crest drawn by eye falls apart the moment somebody scales it.
    - <b>Then traced.</b> Every leaf, the maple at the crown, the arc of the name and the B at the centre, each one a closed contour rather than a shape pasted on top.
    - <b>Filled.</b> One drawing that holds on a blazer, a gate and a favicon, with the colour variations specified rather than improvised by whoever opens the file.
    - "<b>Then the colourways.</b> One drawing, four specified versions: navy on chalk, reversed, Brooklyn blue, and a single colour cut for embroidery and etching. The kit says which goes where, so nobody has to guess on a blazer."
    - <b>Then the system.</b> Two blues, a gold and a working chalk, with the typography assigned by role so a teacher making a flyer cannot get it wrong.
    - <b>Then the site.</b> The programme, the track record and life at the school, in the order a parent actually asks about them.
