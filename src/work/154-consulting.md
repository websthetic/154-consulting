  - This one went through the same order every client project does. The questionnaire first, the decisions written down before anything was drawn, the identity finished before a page was designed. A process that can't survive its own studio has no business being sold to anyone else.
  - The mark sets the number as one continuous stroke, with the five and the four locked together so it reads as a single shape rather than three digits. one.five.four is the long form, kept for the places the brand has to say its name properly. Bone against ink, with ochre as the only colour allowed to interrupt.
  - Before the brand, there's the work sits under the mark because that's the position. The thinking happens before the logo, the logo before the site, and growth only starts paying once both are standing.
  - The site you are reading is the argument rather than a description of it. Every case page sets the mark out, draws it, fills it, shows the variants and the system, then the thing it paid for, in that order, because that is the order the work happened in. If the order were wrong the pages would not hold together, and you would be able to tell.

    - <b>Set out before drawn.</b> Cap height, baseline and a centre axis, with the numerals boxed to the width they have to hold at. A mark going on an invoice and a profile picture gets measured first.
    - <b>Then traced.</b> The 1 leans through the 5 so three figures read as one shape rather than three characters. The counter in the 5 is the only soft curve in the whole mark, and it carries it.
    - <b>Filled, and named.</b> Before the brand sets underneath at the measure the set-out fixed, because it is the argument, not a strapline.
    - <b>Then the variants.</b> The numerals carry it on their own at avatar size, and the full lockup carries the argument everywhere there is room for it. Two, not six. A studio with six logo variants has not decided anything.
    - <b>Then the system.</b> Ink, bone and ochre, with the display face doing all the shouting and nothing else allowed to join in.
