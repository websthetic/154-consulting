explainer:
  heading: A business has one real constraint at a time.
  paragraphs:
    - Everything else is a distraction from it. If demand is the constraint, a better operations manual changes nothing. If delivery is the constraint, more leads make the problem worse rather than better. Most wasted quarters come from working hard on the wrong one, and the work itself was usually fine.
    - In a service business the constraint is nearly always in one of four places. Not enough people know you exist. Enough people arrive and do not buy. Enough people buy and the margin is too thin to be worth it. Or the work is selling fine and you cannot deliver any more of it without breaking something.
    - Finding out which one is a question of looking at the numbers you already have rather than buying new ones. How many enquiries arrived, how many became customers, what each one was worth, and what it cost to get them. Four numbers, and most businesses have never put them next to each other.
  glossary:
    - term: Constraint
      definition: The single bottleneck limiting growth right now. Fix it and the next one appears.
    - term: Offer
      definition: What you sell, how it is packaged, and what is included. Changing this is often cheaper than changing anything else.
    - term: Positioning
      definition: The reason somebody picks you over the alternative, stated plainly enough to repeat.
    - term: Unit economics
      definition: What one customer costs to win and what they are worth. The two numbers every other decision rests on.
    - term: Capacity
      definition: How much work you can deliver well. Growth past this point costs you reputation.
    - term: Lifetime value
      definition: What a customer is worth over the whole relationship, not just the first invoice.

faq:
  - q: How is this different from a marketing agency?
    a: An agency sells you a channel. This starts by finding whether the channel is the problem at all. Sometimes the answer is that your pricing is wrong and no amount of traffic will fix it.
  - q: Is it a one-off or ongoing?
    a: Either. Some clients take a single working session to unstick a decision. Others keep a standing monthly call. There is no minimum term.
  - q: Do I have to buy brand or web work too?
    a: No. The consulting stands on its own, and plenty of clients never buy anything else. If a build is genuinely what you need, we will say so, and if it is not, we will say that too.
