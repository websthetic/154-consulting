explainer:
  heading: The drawing is the smallest part of the job.
  paragraphs:
    - "The drawing is the part everyone talks about and the smallest part of the job. What decides whether a business still looks like itself in two years is the system underneath: which mark goes where, which colour is allowed to carry text, which face does headings and which does body, and what happens when somebody who has never met you needs to make a poster on a Friday."
    - Files matter more than people expect. A printer needs different information from a website, so colour gets specified in CMYK as well as hex, and the logo ships as vector rather than a PNG somebody scaled up. The first time you order signage or a vehicle wrap, a kit that only ever existed as a screenshot costs real money to fix.
    - Guidelines are the part that gets skipped and the part that holds everything together. Clear space, minimum size, what not to do with the mark, how photography should feel. Without them each new piece drifts slightly from the last, nobody notices for a year, and then the brand no longer reads as one thing.
  glossary:
    - term: Logo suite
      definition: "The full set: primary mark, wordmark, icon and monogram, each for a different size and context."
    - term: Wordmark
      definition: Your name set as a designed piece of type, with no symbol attached.
    - term: Monogram
      definition: Initials drawn as a single mark, for small spaces like an app icon or a stamp.
    - term: Lockup
      definition: A fixed arrangement of mark and words that nobody is allowed to rearrange.
    - term: Clear space
      definition: The minimum empty margin around the logo, so it never gets crowded by other elements.
    - term: CMYK
      definition: The four ink values a printer uses. Hex alone is not enough if anything is getting printed.
